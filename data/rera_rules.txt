DUBAI REAL ESTATE REGULATORY AUTHORITY (RERA) — TENANT & LANDLORD RULES
Source: Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007, Decree No. 43 of 2013, and RERA guidelines
Last updated reference: 2024

========================================
SECTION 1 — RENT INCREASE RULES
========================================

Rent increases in Dubai are governed by Decree No. 43 of 2013 and are tied to the RERA Rental Index.

RERA Rent Increase Calculator — Maximum Allowable Increases:
- If current rent is LESS THAN 10% below market rate: NO increase allowed
- If current rent is 11% to 20% below market rate: Maximum increase of 5%
- If current rent is 21% to 30% below market rate: Maximum increase of 10%
- If current rent is 31% to 40% below market rate: Maximum increase of 15%
- If current rent is MORE THAN 40% below market rate: Maximum increase of 20%

Important: The RERA Rental Index is the official reference for market rate. Landlords must use the RERA Smart Rental Index to justify any increase.

Notice requirement for rent increase: The landlord must give the tenant a minimum of 90 days written notice before the rent increase takes effect. Notice must be sent via registered mail or notary public.

Tenant right: If the landlord does not give 90 days notice, the tenant can legally reject the increase for that renewal period.

========================================
SECTION 2 — TENANCY CONTRACT AND EJARI
========================================

All tenancy contracts in Dubai must be registered on the Ejari system (Arabic: "my rent").

Ejari registration:
- Mandatory for all rental contracts in Dubai
- Registration fee: AED 220 (as of 2024)
- Both landlord and tenant can register
- Required to connect DEWA (electricity and water), apply for UAE visa, and renew trade licences

Tenancy contract minimum term: No minimum — can be monthly, 6 months, or annual.
Most common: 1-year contracts.

Contract renewal: Contracts auto-renew under the same terms unless either party gives 90 days notice of non-renewal or change.

========================================
SECTION 3 — SECURITY DEPOSIT
========================================

Standard security deposit: 5% of annual rent for unfurnished properties.
Furnished properties: Up to 10% of annual rent.

The deposit must be returned within 30 days of the tenancy end date, provided:
- No damage beyond normal wear and tear
- All utility bills are settled
- Keys are returned

Landlord deductions from deposit are only allowed for: property damage beyond normal wear and tear, unpaid rent or utility bills, cleaning if property returned in poor condition.

Normal wear and tear (NOT deductible): minor scuffs on walls, worn carpet from regular use, faded paint, minor scratches on flooring.

========================================
SECTION 4 — CHEQUE PAYMENTS
========================================

Number of cheques: There is no law specifying the maximum number of cheques a landlord can request. However, market practice is:
- 1 cheque (full year upfront): common for budget properties, gives tenant leverage to negotiate lower rent
- 2 to 4 cheques: most common market standard
- 6 to 12 cheques: monthly payments, landlord may charge a premium of 5-10% for this flexibility

Landlord cannot legally force you to pay in fewer cheques than what is agreed in the signed contract.

Post-dated cheques: Standard practice in Dubai. Tenant gives the landlord post-dated cheques at contract signing.

Bounced cheque: A criminal offence in Dubai. The landlord can file a police case. Tenant should immediately notify landlord and replace the cheque.

========================================
SECTION 5 — EVICTION RULES
========================================

A landlord can only evict a tenant in these specific cases:

1. Non-payment of rent: Landlord must give 30 days written notice to pay. If unpaid, can apply to Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC).

2. Property use violation: Tenant using property for illegal purposes or against contract terms.

3. Subletting without permission: Tenant sublets without landlord written consent.

4. Major renovations: Landlord requires full property for major structural renovation (must prove to RDSC).

5. Personal use: Landlord wants to use the property for personal use or immediate family. Must give tenant 12 months notice via notary public. Landlord cannot re-rent the property for 2 years after eviction for personal use.

6. Property sale: Landlord selling the property. Must give tenant 12 months notice via notary public.

Tenant protection: If landlord gives improper eviction notice, tenant can challenge it at the RDSC.

========================================
SECTION 6 — MAINTENANCE AND REPAIRS
========================================

Landlord responsibilities:
- Major structural repairs (roof, walls, plumbing systems, electrical systems)
- Air conditioning systems (major repairs and replacements)
- Elevator maintenance in the building
- Common area maintenance

Tenant responsibilities:
- Minor repairs up to AED 500 (general market practice)
- Day-to-day maintenance items (light bulbs, tap washers, minor fixtures)
- Keeping property clean and in good condition
- Reporting major issues to landlord promptly

If landlord refuses to repair: Tenant can report to Dubai Municipality or file at RDSC.

========================================
SECTION 7 — RENTAL DISPUTE SETTLEMENT
========================================

Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC):
- Part of Dubai Land Department (DLD)
- Handles all disputes between landlords and tenants
- Filing fee: 3.5% of annual rent (minimum AED 500, maximum AED 20,000)
- Most cases resolved within 30 to 60 days
- Located in Deira, Dubai. Also accessible online via Dubai REST app.

Common disputes handled: eviction cases, deposit refund disputes, rent increase disagreements, maintenance disputes, contract violations.
